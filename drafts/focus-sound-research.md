# Focus sound: what the research says (checked 28 Nov 2026)

Plain-language summary. Each line links to the real paper or report.

## 1. Binaural beats: small-to-medium effect, mixed results
- **2019 meta-analysis** (Garcia-Argibay, Santed & Reales, *Psychological Research*): 22 studies. Overall medium effect
  on memory, attention, anxiety and pain (g = 0.45). Worked better when people listened **before** the task (or before
  and during), and with **longer** listening. Adding white/pink noise on top didn't change the effect.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/
- **2023 follow-up meta-analysis** (*Psychological Research*): memory and attention only, 15 studies, medium effect
  (g = 0.40), but conflicting results for beta and theta beats. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-022-01706-7
- **2023 systematic review** (Ingendoh, Posny & Heine, *PLOS ONE*): does the brain actually "sync" to the beat? Of 14 EEG
  studies, 5 said yes, 8 said no, 1 mixed. So the "brainwave" explanation is not proven, even where people do better.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548/

## 2. White and pink noise: helps people with attention trouble, can hurt others
- **2024 meta-analysis** (Nigg and colleagues): people with ADHD or ADHD symptoms did a little better with white/pink
  noise (g = 0.25). People without ADHD did a little **worse** (g = -0.21). No studies of brown noise were found.
  Many studies lacked good control conditions. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11283987/
- Background idea (Söderlund 2007, "moderate brain arousal"): some brains need more outside stimulation to focus.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17683456/
- **What this means for you:** noise might help or hurt, depending on your brain. That's why the player now tests
  "no noise" too.

## 3. Pink noise during sleep: real effect, but a different use
- **Ngo et al. 2013** (*Neuron*): short pink-noise clicks timed exactly to deep-sleep brain waves (measured with EEG)
  improved next-day word memory. This needs live EEG timing; a steady noise track doesn't do the same thing.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3679648/

## 4. 40 Hz gamma: early medical research, not a focus tool (yet)
- MIT (Li-Huei Tsai's lab): 40 Hz light + sound was safe in small studies and showed hints of benefit in early
  Alzheimer's (15 people in the pilot). The authors say this is not proof it works. Nothing shows it helps healthy
  people focus. https://news.mit.edu/2022/small-studies-40hz-sensory-stimulation-confirm-safety-suggest-alzheimers-benefits-1213

## 5. Little or no research
- Isochronic and monaural beats: very few good studies.
- Green and grey noise: mostly marketing names.
- Solfeggio / 432 Hz / 528 Hz: no scientific support.

## How the player uses this
1. **Personal testing beats general claims.** Effects are small and vary by person, so the player rates each session and
   finds what works for *you*.
2. **It includes "control" setups** (no tone, and silence) so you can see whether the beats or the noise actually help.
3. **Optional 5-minute warm-up** of tone before the timer, because listening before the task did better in the studies.
4. Honest language: "may help", no medical claims.
